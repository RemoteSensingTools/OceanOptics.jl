Inelastic processes

Fluorescence, water Raman, and CDOM fluorescence packaged as kernels consumed by a two-pass RT solver (Fell 1997 §2.2.9), and the light absorbed per layer and component that drives them.

Conventions

Conversion units. Every process factorizes into a conversion coefficient κ(λ′) =excitation_absorption [1/m] and a redistribution density f(λ′, λ) =emission [1/nm] with ∫ f dλ = 1. conversion_units states whether κ counts photons or energy, and the two kernels follow by dispatch:

conversion_units(p)energy kernel K_E (radiance_source_kernel)photon kernel K_N (inelastic_coefficient)processes
PhotonConversionκ f λ′/λκ fIsotropicFluorescence, CDOMFluorescence; WaterRaman(conversion = PhotonConversion())
EnergyConversionκ fκ f λ/λ′WaterRaman (default)

In both cases K_N = K_E λ/λ′, and the power emitted per unit volume and per nm of emission wavelength is

\[4\pi \bar S(\lambda) = \int K_E(\lambda', \lambda)\, E_0(\lambda')\, d\lambda'\]

with E₀ the spectral scalar irradiance [W m⁻² nm⁻¹]. inelastic_emission integrates the same kernel over a layer.

Fluorescence is a photon conversion: κ = φ Σ_k η_k a_k(λ′) with the quantum yield φ in photons emitted per photon delivered to the fluorescing pigment, so ∫ K_N dλ = κ and the emitted energy carries the photon-energy ratio λ′/λ.

Water Raman is an energy conversion by default: the Raman scattering coefficient b_R(λ′) = b_R(λ_ref) (λ_ref/λ′)^n (defaults 2.7·10⁻⁴ m⁻¹ at 488 nm, n = 5.5) is defined so that b_R(λ′) E₀(λ′) is the power emitted over the whole Raman band per unit volume and per nm of excitation wavelength,

\[\int K_E(\lambda', \lambda)\, d\lambda = b_R(\lambda') ,\]

with no factor λ′/λ. This is the definition of Mobley et al. (1993, Appl. Opt. 32, 7484; Problem 7 and Eqs. A3–A4): in Problem 7 all light Raman scattered at 417 nm appears at 486 nm and the source of radiance is b_R E₀/(4π) times the phase function. WaterRaman(conversion = PhotonConversion()) treats the same number as a photon coefficient instead (∫ K_N dλ = b_R); its energy source is lower by the band mean of λ′/λ: 0.881 at 350 nm excitation, 0.858 at 417 nm, 0.834 at 488 nm, 0.796 at 600 nm.

Raman angular distribution and depolarization. WaterRaman stores the depolarization ratio ρ for linearly polarized excitation (linear_depolarization, default 0.17 from Mobley et al. 1993, Eq. 9). The phase function is

\[p_R(\cos\Theta) = \frac{1}{4\pi}\left[1 + \beta_2 P_2(\cos\Theta)\right], \qquad \beta_2 = \frac{1-\rho}{2(1+2\rho)} = 0.3097 \ \text{for}\ \rho = 0.17 ,\]

identical to Mobley's Eq. 9, (3/16π) (1+3ρ)/(1+2ρ) [1 + (1−ρ)/(1+3ρ) cos²Θ]. The ratio for unpolarized (natural) excitation is ρ_n = 2ρ/(1+ρ) (0.2906; natural_depolarization), and β₂ = (1−ρ_n)/(2+ρ_n) is the same number. raman_phase returns the RayleighWaterPhase with depolarization ρ_n, whose polarized Greek expansion is the Raman phase matrix.

Raman loss. The bundled water absorption models do not add a separate Raman loss; whether b_R(λ′) is added to the attenuation at the excitation wavelength is the solver's convention and must match the reference it is compared with (Mobley et al. 1993 take it to be contained in the Smith & Baker absorption).

Pigment partitions outside their tables. The pigment spectra of ProspectPigments (400–800 nm) and Bidigare1990Pigments (400–750 nm) do not cover the UV. By default (outside =HoldEdgeShares()) the shares of the nearest tabulated wavelength apply outside the table, so absorption below 400 nm is attributed to the pigment groups as at 400 nm and chlorophyll_fluorescence with excitation_range = (350, 700) is excited continuously across 400 nm; with all transfer efficiencies equal to 1 its excitation equals that of the unpartitioned phytoplankton. The UV shares are an extrapolation, not data. A partition built with outside =UnassignedOutsideTable() puts all absorption outside its table in :unassigned (η = 0); the excitation then steps to zero just below 400 nm, which removes 21.9 % of the 350–700 nm excitation integral at Chl = 0.5 mg m⁻³. Under both rules Bidigare1990Pigments assigns nothing above about 732 nm, where all its group spectra vanish. The default excitation_range of chlorophyll_fluorescence is (400, 700) nm.

Absorption that excites chlorophyll fluorescence. With Bricaud1998 the absorption of Phytoplankton is the particulate absorption a_p of Case-1 water, detritus included; the fluorescence excitation and the pigment shares are fractions of that total.

OceanOptics.TabulatedEmission — Type
struct TabulatedEmission{FT<:AbstractFloat} <: AbstractSpectralRedistribution{FT<:AbstractFloat}

Emission spectrum tabulated in wavelength, independent of excitation: photon density f(λ) [1/nm], interpolated linearly between the tabulated points and zero outside them.

Fields

  • λ::Vector{FT} where FT<:AbstractFloat: Emission wavelengths [nm], strictly increasing

  • density::Vector{FT} where FT<:AbstractFloat: Photon density [1/nm] at each λ, with unit trapezoidal integral

  • source::String: Provenance: citation, table and any transformation applied

Construction

TabulatedEmission(λ, shape; units = :photons, source = "")

λ [nm] strictly increasing; shape finite, non-negative and not all zero, in any scale. shape is a spectral shape: it is divided by its trapezoidal integral, which makes the interpolated density integrate to exactly one. With units = :energy, shape is an energy spectrum (as most measured emission spectra are) and is first converted to photon numbers, ∝ shape · λ. Tabulate down to zero at both ends: the density drops to zero outside the table.

OceanOptics.chloroplast_emission — Function
chloroplast_emission([FT = Float64]; psi_share = nothing) -> TabulatedEmission

Return the chlorophyll fluorescence emission spectrum at the photosystem (chloroplast) level, before any reabsorption.

The spectra are those used for leaves by Fluspect (data/fluspect_chloroplast_emission.csv, 640–851 nm):

  • psi_share = nothing: Fluspect's combined spectrum phi (the default of SCOPE 2), with maxima at 685 and 739 nm;
  • psi_share = w ∈ [0, 1]: the photosystem mixture w · phiI + (1 − w) · phiII of the separate PSI (maximum at 728 nm) and PSII (682 nm) spectra, with w the share of emitted photons from PSI.

The spectra are photon densities per nm. Reabsorption by water and by the pigments themselves is left to the radiative transfer: the emission shape contains none.

OceanOptics.WavenumberShiftEmission — Type
struct WavenumberShiftEmission{FT<:AbstractFloat} <: AbstractSpectralRedistribution{FT<:AbstractFloat}

Gaussian emission band in wavenumber space, centered at a fixed offset from the excitation wavenumber. Used for water Raman scattering, where emission wavenumber ν = ν' − Δν with Δν ≈ 3400 cm⁻¹ (Haltrin & Kattawar 1991/1993).

Construction:

# Water Raman: 3400 cm⁻¹ Stokes shift, 85 cm⁻¹ Gaussian σ
WavenumberShiftEmission{Float64}(shift_cm_inv = 3400.0, σ_cm_inv = 85.0)

Fields

  • shift_cm_inv::AbstractFloat: Stokes shift Δν = ν_excitation − ν_emission [1/cm]

  • σ_cm_inv::AbstractFloat: Gaussian standard deviation in wavenumber [1/cm]

Mathematical note

The stored Gaussian is normalized in wavenumber (∫ g(ν) dν = 1); redistribution applies the Jacobian |dν/dλ| = 10⁷/λ² so the emitted density f(λ', λ) is also normalized in wavelength, satisfying ∫ f dλ = 1.

OceanOptics.WaterRaman — Type
struct WaterRaman{FT<:AbstractFloat, U<:AbstractConversionUnits} <: AbstractOceanInelasticProcess{FT<:AbstractFloat}

Water Raman scattering as an inelastic process: a Gaussian emission band in wavenumber centred at ν' − shift_cm_inv, with the scattering coefficient

$b_R(λ') = b_R(λ_\mathrm{ref}) (λ_\mathrm{ref}/λ')^n$ [1/m].

Convention of b_R

The type parameter U (field conversion) states what b_R converts (see conversion_units):

  • EnergyConversion (default): b_R(λ') E₀(λ') is the power emitted over the whole Raman band per unit volume and per nm of excitation wavelength. The energy kernel is K_E(λ', λ) = b_R(λ') f_R(λ', λ) with ∫ K_E dλ = b_R(λ'), and the photon kernel is K_N = K_E λ/λ'. This is the definition of Mobley et al. (1993, Appl. Opt. 32, 7484), whose Problem 7 source is b_R E₀/(4π) for the 417 → 486 nm transfer.
  • PhotonConversion: b_R counts photons, K_N = b_R f_R with ∫ K_N dλ = b_R(λ'), and K_E = K_N λ'/λ. Its energy source is 12 % (excitation at 350 nm) to 24 % (700 nm) below that of the energy convention for the same b_R.

Angular distribution

$p_R(\cos Θ) = \frac{1}{4π}\left[1 + β_2 P_2(\cos Θ)\right]$, $β_2 = \frac{1 - ρ}{2 (1 + 2ρ)}$

(Mobley et al. 1993, Eq. 9), with ρ = linear_depolarization the depolarization ratio for linearly polarized excitation; β₂ = 0.3097 for ρ = 0.17. See raman_phase for the phase function and phase matrix.

Fields

  • shift_cm_inv::AbstractFloat: Stokes shift between excitation and emission wavenumbers [1/cm]

  • σ_cm_inv::AbstractFloat: Gaussian standard deviation of the emission band in wavenumber [1/cm]

  • cross_section_ref::AbstractFloat: Raman scattering coefficient b_R at the reference wavelength [1/m]

  • cross_section_λ_ref::AbstractFloat: Reference wavelength of the power law [nm]

  • cross_section_n::AbstractFloat: Exponent n of b_R(λ') = b_ref (λ_ref / λ')^n

  • linear_depolarization::AbstractFloat: Depolarization ratio ρ for linearly polarized excitation, observed at 90°

  • excitation_range::Tuple{FT, FT} where FT<:AbstractFloat: Excitation-wavelength integration window [nm]

  • conversion::AbstractConversionUnits: Quantity that b_R converts: EnergyConversion() or PhotonConversion()

Parameters and their sources

fielddefaultsource
shift_cm_inv, σ_cm_inv3400, 85 cm⁻¹single-Gaussian band attributed to Haltrin & Kattawar (1993, Appl. Opt. 32, 5356)
cross_section_ref at cross_section_λ_ref2.7·10⁻⁴ m⁻¹ at 488 nmattributed to Bartlett et al. (1998, JGR 103, 17875); the HydroLight runs of Loisel et al. (2023) are reproduced with 2.6·10⁻⁴ m⁻¹

OceanOptics.chlorophyll_fluorescence — Function
chlorophyll_fluorescence(phyto::Phytoplankton; quantum_yield,
                         carotenoid_transfer = nothing, phycobilin_transfer = 1,
                         photoprotective_transfer = 0, transfer = (;), emission = chloroplast_emission(FT),
                         excitation_range = (400, 700)) -> IsotropicFluorescence

Build the chlorophyll-a fluorescence process of phyto, with one excitation pathway per absorption part of phyto.

partdefault η
:total (no pigment partition)1: all phytoplankton absorption
chlorophylls1
carotenoids (photosynthetic, or Prospect's lumped carotenoids)carotenoid_transfer: required when the partition has them
photoprotective carotenoidsphotoprotective_transfer (0)
phycobilinsphycobilin_transfer
:unassigned0

Phycobilisomes are a photosystem II antenna; the default phycobilin_transfer = 1 is a modelling choice. Photoprotective carotenoids dissipate the energy they absorb instead of passing it on to chlorophyll, hence photoprotective_transfer = 0.

transfer overrides η for named parts, e.g. (photosynthetic_carotenoids = 0.8, photoprotective_carotenoids = 0.0) for Bidigare1990Pigments. carotenoid_transfer = 0 is chlorophyll-only excitation (as in Fluspect, whose excitation is the chlorophyll share of leaf absorption); 1 makes carotenoids a lossless antenna; values between give the share that is passed on rather than quenched.

The pigment spectra of the partitions start at 400 nm. With the default HoldEdgeShares rule the shares at 400 nm apply below, so excitation in the UV (excitation_range starting below 400 nm) is attributed to the pigments as at 400 nm and nothing is dropped; a partition built with outside = UnassignedOutsideTable() has no excitation outside its table. Without a partition, all absorption within excitation_range excites. With Bricaud1998 the absorption is the particulate absorption, detritus included, and all of it enters the excitation.

quantum_yield (φ) has no default: in situ values range over roughly 0.01–0.05 (Loisel et al. 2023, who use HydroLight's 0.02). The default emission is Fluspect's chloroplast-level spectrum (chloroplast_emission), the default excitation window PAR. phyto must also be a constituent of every layer the process is in; see layer_optics.

Example

phyto = Phytoplankton(Chl = 0.5, pigments = ProspectPigments(carotenoid_ratio = 0.3))
chl_only = chlorophyll_fluorescence(phyto; quantum_yield = 0.02, carotenoid_transfer = 0.0)
antenna  = chlorophyll_fluorescence(phyto; quantum_yield = 0.02, carotenoid_transfer = 0.6)

OceanOptics.AbstractOceanInelasticProcess — Type
abstract type AbstractOceanInelasticProcess{FT<:Real}

Root of the inelastic-source hierarchy.

A concrete subtype (IsotropicFluorescence, WaterRaman, CDOMFluorescence) describes one excitation-to-emission channel and implements the primitives of the factorization of Fell (1997): excitation_absorption (p, λ'), emission(p, λ', λ), excitation_range(p), is_isotropic(p) and conversion_units(p), which states whether the excitation coefficient counts photons or energy.

OceanOptics.PhotonConversion — Type
PhotonConversion()

Conversion units of a process whose coefficient a^I(λ′) [1/m] counts photons: a^I(λ′) f^I(λ′, λ) is the number of photons emitted per nm at λ per unit photon fluence at λ′.

The emitted energy carries the photon-energy ratio λ′/λ. Fluorescence, defined by a quantum yield in photons per photon, is a photon conversion.

OceanOptics.EnergyConversion — Type
EnergyConversion()

Conversion units of a process whose coefficient a^I(λ′) [1/m] counts energy: a^I(λ′) f^I(λ′, λ) E₀(λ′) is the power emitted per unit volume and per nm at λ for the spectral scalar irradiance E₀(λ′) [W m⁻² nm⁻¹], with no photon-energy ratio.

OceanOptics.radiance_source_kernel — Function
radiance_source_kernel(p, λ_prime, λ) -> FT

Return the energy kernel K_E(λ', λ) [1/m/nm] that converts excitation spectral irradiance into emitted energy.

The power emitted per unit volume and per nm at λ is ∫ K_E(λ', λ) E₀(λ') dλ', with E₀ the spectral scalar irradiance [W m⁻² nm⁻¹] (total intensity integrated over solid angle, direct and diffuse). For an isotropic process the volume source of spectral radiance is

\[J(\lambda) = \frac{1}{4\pi} \int K_E(\lambda', \lambda) E_0(\lambda')\, d\lambda'\]

With κ = excitation_absorption(p, λ') and f = emission(p, λ', λ), K_E = κ f λ'/λ for a PhotonConversion (fluorescence; λ'/λ is the ratio of photon energies) and K_E = κ f for an EnergyConversion (see conversion_units). For the default WaterRaman, an energy conversion, ∫ K_E dλ = b_R(λ'). inelastic_coefficient is the photon kernel K_N = K_E λ/λ'.

On a discrete grid the solver integrates with its own bin weights and, in wavenumber, the Jacobian |dλ/dν| = 10⁷/ν².

OceanOptics.absorbed_power — Function
absorbed_power(o::OceanLayerOptics, E₀; roundoff_rtol = √eps) -> Matrix

Return the spectral power absorbed by each absorption component of layer o, A[c, k] = τ_c(λ_k) · E₀[k].

E₀[k] is the layer-mean spectral scalar irradiance at o.λ[k] ((1/Δz) ∫ E₀ dz over the layer, total intensity over all directions, direct and diffuse). Rows follow o.absorbers; units are those of E₀ (W m⁻² nm⁻¹ ⇒ absorbed W m⁻² nm⁻¹ per layer).

E₀ must be finite and non-negative. Negative entries at round-off level, E₀[k] ≥ −roundoff_rtol · maximum(abs, E₀), are treated as zero; more negative entries throw an ArgumentError. The default roundoff_rtol is √eps(T) with T the float type of E₀ (1.5e-8 in Float64); roundoff_rtol = 0 rejects every negative entry. The same rule and keyword apply in absorbed_photons, absorbed_par and inelastic_emission.

absorbed_photons(o::OceanLayerOptics, E₀) -> Matrix

Return absorbed_power in photon units: µmol photons m⁻² s⁻¹ nm⁻¹ per absorption component, for E₀ in W m⁻² nm⁻¹ (photon energy hc/λ).

OceanOptics.absorbed_par — Function
absorbed_par(o::OceanLayerOptics, E₀; range = (400, 700), roundoff_rtol = √eps) -> Vector

Return the photosynthetically available radiation absorbed by each absorption component of layer o: absorbed_photons integrated over range [nm], in µmol photons m⁻² s⁻¹.

The integral is that of the linear interpolant on the layer grid (trapezoidal rule, end intervals clipped at the range limits), so the grid must span range but need not contain its limits. Entries follow o.absorbers; see absorber_index.

For phytoplankton with a pigment partition this gives the PAR absorbed by each pigment group, e.g. by chlorophyll.

OceanOptics.inelastic_emission — Function
inelastic_emission(o::OceanLayerOptics, j, E₀, λ_em;
                   range = excitation_range(p), roundoff_rtol = √eps) -> Vector

Return the spectral power [W m⁻² nm⁻¹] emitted in all directions by layer o through its j-th inelastic component (process p), at emission wavelengths λ_em [nm]:

P(λ) = Δz ∫ radiance_source_kernel(p, λ′, λ) Ē₀(λ′) dλ′
     = ∫ τᴵ(λ′) Ē₀(λ′) f(λ′, λ) λ′/λ dλ′      (photon conversion: fluorescence)
     = ∫ τᴵ(λ′) Ē₀(λ′) f(λ′, λ) dλ′           (energy conversion: default water Raman)

according to conversion_units(p), with τᴵ from excitation_optical_depth (the layer's stored absorption components) and E₀ the layer-mean scalar irradiance on the layer grid (as in absorbed_power), integrated over range as in absorbed_par. For an isotropic process the volume source of radiance is P / (4π Δz) per unit emission wavelength.

For fluorescence, the photons emitted equal the quantum yield times the photons the excitation components deliver, ∫ P λ/(hc) dλ = φ Σ_k η_k ∫ A_k λ′/(hc) dλ′ (to the extent λ_em covers the emission band); reabsorption of the emission is left to the radiative transfer.

OceanOptics.excitation_optical_depth — Function
excitation_optical_depth(o::OceanLayerOptics, j) -> Vector

Return the excitation optical depth τᴵ(λ) = aᴵ(λ) Δz of the layer's j-th inelastic component on the layer grid.

For a process with excitation pathways (fluorescence) it is formed from the stored absorption components, quantum_yield(p) · Σ_k efficiency[k] · absorbers[excitation[k]].τ, which equals Δz · excitation_absorption(p, λ); otherwise (water Raman) it is Δz · excitation_absorption(p, λ).

OceanOptics.absorber_index — Function
absorber_index(o::OceanLayerOptics, label, part = :total) -> Int

Return the index in o.absorbers of the absorption component with label and part, e.g. absorber_index(o, "Phytoplankton", :chlorophyll).

The same index names the rows of absorbed_power and the entries of absorbed_par. Throws if there is no such component or more than one.
