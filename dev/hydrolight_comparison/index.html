Comparison with HydroLight IOPs

Loisel et al. (2023) published the inherent optical properties used as input for 29 880 HydroLight v5.0 simulations: 3320 combinations of phytoplankton, non-algal particle and CDOM properties plus pure seawater, at 350–750 nm in 5 nm steps (ESSD article, Dryad dataset, CC0). This page compares OceanOptics.jl with those inputs.

The file data/reference/loisel2023_hydrolight_iops.csv holds the extract used here and in the test suite: the pure-seawater coefficients (the same in every scenario; derived as total minus non-water) and three scenarios at the 5th, 50th and 95th percentile of total a(440). Its header records the source file and checksum. Database values are single precision, with totals rounded to about five significant figures.

The paper's pure-seawater assumptions:

QuantityHydroLight input (Loisel et al. 2023, §2)
a_w(λ)IOCCG Protocol Series (2018) composite: Morel et al. (2007) 340–415 nm, Pope & Fry (1997) 420–725 nm, Kou et al. (1993) 730–750 nm
b_w(λ)Zhang et al. (2009) at 18 °C and salinity 35
particlesFournier-Forand phase functions, b_b/b = 0.01 (phytoplankton), 0.018 (non-algal)

Pure-seawater scattering

OceanOptics' port of the Zhang, Hu & He (2009) model, evaluated at the same temperature and salinity, reproduces the HydroLight b_w to the precision of the stored data over the whole range. Morel's (1974) power law is 7–16 % higher.

zhh   = scattering(water(), λ) ./ ref["bw"]
morel = scattering(water(b = Morel1974()), λ) ./ ref["bw"]

Pure-water absorption

The default absorption model, IOCCG2018, is the composite HydroLight used here — its 350–750 nm table is taken from this database — continued above 750 nm with Kou et al. (1993), the composite's own source at 730–750 nm. The individual primary tables bundled with OceanOptics compare as follows (each drawn over its own range; at 22 °C, where no temperature correction applies):

Pope & Fry is the composite between 420 and 725 nm; Smith & Baker's "clearest natural water" values are several times higher in the UV, which is why they are no longer used in the default (the previous default fell back to them below 380 nm); Kou et al. (1993) supplies the composite's 730–750 nm values to within 1 %.

OceanOptics applies the Röttgers et al. (2014) temperature correction from 550 nm upward, relative to the tables' 22 °C reference; HydroLight's tabulated values carry no correction. At the database's 18 °C the correction changes a_w by up to a few percent near the 740 nm O–H band:

δ = absorption(water(), λ) ./ absorption(water(T = 22.0), λ) .- 1
@printf("largest change at 18 °C: %.2f %% at %.0f nm\n", 100minimum(δ), λ[argmin(δ)])
largest change at 18 °C: -2.35 % at 735 nm

Bulk IOPs through the tabulated route

The three scenarios can be built from tabulated coefficients: the bulk absorption as a TabulatedAbsorber with total = true, and pure water, phytoplankton and non-algal particles as TabulatedScatterers with their phase functions. The resulting single-scattering albedo and backscatter fraction match the HydroLight totals to the precision of the stored values.

cases  = ("clear", "median", "turbid")
series = Series[]
for (k, case) in enumerate(cases)
    c(v) = ref["$(case)_$v"]
    layer = OceanLayer(0.0, 1.0, AbstractOceanConstituent{Float64}[
        TabulatedAbsorber(λ, c("a"); total = true),
        TabulatedScatterer(λ, ref["bw"], RayleighWaterPhase{Float64}()),
        TabulatedScatterer(λ, c("bph"), FournierForandPhase{Float64}(backscatter_fraction = 0.01)),
        TabulatedScatterer(λ, c("bd"), FournierForandPhase{Float64}(backscatter_fraction = 0.018))])
    opt  = layer_optics(layer, λ; ℓ_max = 16)
    ϖ_hl = c("b") ./ (c("a") .+ c("b"))
    ϖ, B = single_scattering_albedo(opt), backscatter_fraction(opt)
    @printf("%-7s max |Δϖ| = %.1e   max |ΔB/B| = %.1e\n", case,
            maximum(abs, ϖ .- ϖ_hl), maximum(abs, B ./ (c("bb") ./ c("b")) .- 1))
    push!(series, Series("$case: OceanOptics", λ, ϖ; slot = k - 1))
    push!(series, Series("$case: HydroLight", λ, ϖ_hl; points = true, slot = k - 1))
end
linechart(series; xlabel = "wavelength λ [nm]", ylabel = "single-scattering albedo ϖ",

What this establishes

  • The ZhangHu2009 seawater-scattering port agrees with HydroLight's input at 18 °C and salinity 35 within the data's precision (test/test_hydrolight_reference.jl checks 1 × 10⁻⁴).
  • The bundled Pope & Fry table matches over 420–725 nm, and Kou et al. (1993) over 730–750 nm within 1 %; the default IOCCG2018 model is the HydroLight composite by construction.
  • Bulk single-scattering albedo and backscatter fraction are reproduced through the tabulated route, with Fournier-Forand particles on the Mobley et al. (2002) family that HydroLight uses.
  • Particle phase-function shapes are not part of the database and are not compared here.
